## What's new in 4.0.1

One change, for the picture in motion.

### Keep the HUD sharp is now off unless you switch it on

4.0 switched **Keep the HUD sharp** on for everyone. Within hours we heard, and saw ourselves, that the
picture had become softer in motion, that the edges of moving things and of the character broke up when
the camera panned, and that thin things such as poles cut in and out. Going back to 3.2.10 made it go away.

The reason: the feature keeps a pixel as the console drew it when the pixel is the same in two frames in a
row. That is true of a HUD, but it is also true, by chance, of a great many pixels in a moving game picture
(road, walls, skin, anything smooth or motion blurred). Those were held back while the rest of the
picture moved. Measured afterwards on moving test pictures of the kind real games produce, in which
nothing stands still, it had overwritten between 5 and 38 % of every generated frame. The drawn test
pictures it was first measured on did not show this.

- From 4.0.1 the option is **off** for everyone who has not switched it on themselves. With it off, the
  picture is made exactly as in 3.2.10, and everything else from 4.0 stays.
- It is still in *Quick setup → Keep the HUD sharp* for whoever liked what it did for a HUD, with a line
  that says what it can do to a moving picture.
- For a HUD, the game presets, HUD protection and Live HUD Lock are unchanged. They know where the HUD is,
  or watch it over time, and leave the rest of the picture alone.

If you switched the option off yourself in 4.0, nothing changes for you.

### How this was checked

The change is a default and a line of text. The automatic tests ran in the build pipeline on a software
renderer. The measurement above was made with the feature's own rule on generated test pictures, not on a
graphics card. That the picture in motion is back to what 3.2.10 gave was reported by users who switched
the option off or went back to 3.2.10; it was not measured by us on a real card.
