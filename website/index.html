Your console at 60 or 120 FPS, straight from your capture card.

PushFPS reads the picture from your capture card and adds generated frames in real time on your PC. Nothing changes on the console or in the game. Free and open source, for Windows 11.

How it works

A 30 FPS game repeats every picture twice inside the console's 60 Hz signal. PushFPS keeps the real pictures and fills the gaps with new frames on your graphics card.

  1. Console

    PS5, Xbox Series or Switch 2, set to 60 Hz output (120 Hz if your capture card captures 120 Hz).

  2. HDMI

  3. Capture card

    Any card Windows sees as a camera, USB or PCIe.

  4. USB / PCIe

  5. PushFPS

    Finds the real frames, then generates the ones in between on your GPU.

  6. 60 / 120 FPS

  7. Your monitor

    Play at the frame rate you picked. Optionally stream it with OBS.

Smoother, not faster

Generated frames make motion look smoother. They don't make the game react faster: your inputs still land at the game's own pace, plus the capture card's delay and up to one game frame (about 33 ms at 30 FPS). PushFPS measures its own part and can show it on screen. For competitive games, play on the console's own display path.

Made for console players

Three choices instead of hundreds, its own frame generation, and tools that tune and protect the picture for you.

PushFlow: our own frame generation

PushFlow runs RIFE, an open neural network for in-between frames, through DirectML, so it works on NVIDIA, AMD and Intel graphics cards alike. Pick 2×, 3× or 4× and a tier: Fast, Balanced or Quality. It never waits on the CPU for a frame, and if your card can't keep up with a tier it steps down and tells you. It's in Professional mode and in streaming mode, and the optimiser can measure it on your own game.

Setup guide: the best settings

Where to start in PushFPS, how to tune it from there, and what to set on your console, your capture card and in Windows. Also as a text file: PushFPS-Setup-Guide.txt, attached to every release.

1. Start here

  1. Get the game on screen

    Connect the capture card and start your game, so PushFPS can see the real picture. Capture at 60 FPS, even for a 30 FPS game (why).

  2. Fill in the quick setup

    Your console, game frame rate 30 (40 or 60 for those modes), and the target for your monitor from the table below: 60 on a 60 Hz monitor, 120 FPS on 120 Hz and faster. Pick the game preset if it's listed.

  3. Keep these on

    Smooth frame pacing and Live HUD Lock. Adaptive multiplier for games that dip. Lowest latency for action games. AI enhancement off to start. Frame Pacing Repair stays off unless a game locked at 30 or 40 FPS still judders.

  4. Optimise for my PC

    With the game on screen, goal Balanced. PushFPS tests every method your PC has and keeps the best. Undo puts everything back.

Which target for your monitor

Pick a target your monitor's refresh rate divides evenly, so every frame stays on screen equally long. That's why 120 beats 144: 144 isn't a whole multiple of 30 or 60. Match monitor sets the target to your monitor's own rate.

4. Capture card settings

With thanks to Likely7, Veyra's developer: the delay figures and card picks below are his, checked against the makers' specs and corrected where needed.

  • Frame rate60 FPSAlso for a 30 FPS game: each new picture reaches the PC sooner than with a 30 FPS capture. Half the captured frames are then repeats (AABBCC), so PushFPS removes them before generating frames. The quick setup and Optimise for my PC do this for you; in Pro mode it's Remove duplicate frames (Automatic removal or 60→30). 120 FPS only if your card captures 120 Hz.
  • FormatNV12, or P010 for HDRUncompressed. Avoid MJPEG where you can: it's compressed and has to be decoded. On a 5 Gbps card, 4K60 only exists as MJPEG (the Elgato 4K S, for example).

NVIDIA Video Frame Generation: Medium or High?

For 30 FPS games it gives the least ghosting and wobble of the methods (RTX 40/50; RTX 30 experimental). High is the cleanest but heavy: Veyra's developer measured about 20 ms per generated 4K frame on an RTX 5070. That fits 2× (one new frame per 33 ms game frame) but not 4×, which needs three. Medium costs about a third to a quarter of High and still beats the other methods at 30 FPS, so it's the default. In between, High+Medium uses High only for the middle frame of each pair. At 2×, VFG auto regulation drops a pair to the next tier when it can't keep up, instead of dropping frames. Find these in Pro mode under Frame generation, or let Optimise for my PC test what your card keeps up with.

From the latest release on GitHub.

  1. Download and extract

    Get PushFPS-…-win64.zip from the latest release and extract it anywhere.

  2. Run the installer

    Double-click Install-PushFPS.cmd. It downloads Veyra's runtime package from Veyra's own GitHub release, checks it, and installs PushFPS to your user folder with Start menu and desktop shortcuts.

  3. Connect your console

    Console HDMI into the capture card, the card into your PC. Set the console to 60 Hz, or 120 Hz if your card captures 120 Hz. On PlayStation, turn off Enable HDCP in Settings, System, HDMI, or the picture stays black.

  4. Pick your frame rate

    Open PushFPS, click Connect on the capture card and fill in the quick setup, or let Optimise for my PC do it. Press F11 for fullscreen.

Portable: run Install-PushFPS.cmd -InstallDir D:\PushFPS -NoShortcuts to keep PushFPS and its settings in one folder of your choice. Every release lists SHA-256 checksums in SHA256SUMS.txt.

Windows warns about an unknown publisher? PushFPS isn't code signed yet. If you downloaded it from the PushFPS GitHub releases, choose More info, then Run anyway. To update, accept the new version when PushFPS offers it (signed, one click), or run the newer installer; your settings are kept either way. To uninstall, run Install-PushFPS.cmd -Uninstall.

Questions

Not answered here? Ask on the PushFPS Discord.

Is PushFPS really free?

Yes. It's free and open source under the GPL-3.0, like Veyra. There are no accounts, ads or paid tiers.

Does it change my console or the game?

No. PushFPS only processes the video coming out of the capture card. Nothing is installed on the console and the game isn't touched.

Does it add input lag?

A little. To place a frame between two real frames, PushFPS waits for the second one: up to one game frame, about 33 ms at 30 FPS, on top of your capture card's own delay. Motion looks smoother, but the game doesn't react faster. Turn on Lowest latency for every setting that cuts delay without costing picture quality, and Show latency on screen to see PushFPS's part measured, and use Measure total delay for the whole chain. If your capture card captures 120 Hz (passthrough doesn't count), a 120 Hz console output gets each frame to the PC about 8 ms sooner.

Do I need an NVIDIA card?

No. PushFlow, PushFPS's own frame generation (2× to 4×), and AMD FSR frame generation (2×) run on any DirectX 12 card, and ANR by Apex Compute supports GTX 16 and newer, Radeon RX 5000 and newer and Intel Arc. AMD cards also get AMD FRC through the AMD driver. NVIDIA RTX 40 and 50 cards get the most options, including up to 8× frame generation.

AI picture enhancement says the package lacks the runtime components. Why?

AI picture enhancement on NVIDIA RTX and Radeon RX 7000 / 9000 cards uses community-built runtime files. The installer treats them as experimental extras: it asks, and the default is no. Run Install-PushFPS.cmd again and answer y when it asks about the experimental extras. Your settings are kept, and updates from inside PushFPS keep the extras. Frame generation doesn't need them.

Which capture cards work?

Any card that Windows shows as a camera and that sends 1080p at 60 FPS without compression. The Elgato 4K X, HD60 X and 4K S are tested, and users report the Elgato HD60 S, Elgato HD60 Pro, AVerMedia Live Gamer Ultra 2.1 and ezcap Game Link Raw working. The app shows each card's status and has a report form for untested cards.

Can I use it without a capture card?

Yes. PS5 streaming (Remote Play over your home network), PC streaming from a Sunshine host and Xbox streaming are built in. A capture card still gives the sharpest picture and the lowest delay.

Does it work with 60 FPS games?

Yes. Set the game frame rate to 60 and pick 120 FPS or Match monitor.

Can I stream or record it?

Yes. PushFPS records, keeps an instant replay and goes live itself: to Twitch, YouTube, Kick, TikTok, Facebook, Trovo or your own RTMP server, several at once. Streaming mode lays it out like OBS, with a layout editor, platform presets and an audio mixer for game sound, your microphone, desktop audio and an extra input. The OBS Studio plugin adds the boosted picture to your OBS scenes if you prefer OBS.

Is there a macOS or Linux version?

Yes, as a preview. It has PushFPS's own frame generation, Frame Pacing Repair and Live HUD Lock on Vulkan, OpenGL or Metal, without NVIDIA, AMD or Intel components (those exist only for Windows). Linux comes as an AppImage, macOS as one app for Apple Silicon and Intel. Recording and going live are Windows-only for now.

Does PushFPS collect any data?

No. There's no telemetry or analytics. It only goes online for the update check on GitHub, when you share a clip to your own Discord webhook, when you connect a streaming source and when you go live (only to the platforms you chose). See the privacy policy.

Where do I get help or report a problem?

Ask on the PushFPS Discord, where other players and the developer answer. For a bug, Settings, Report a problem in the app copies diagnostic information and opens GitHub Issues. Read it before pasting.
