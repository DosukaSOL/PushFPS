PushFPS setup guide: the best settings to start from
====================================================

PushFPS is built on Veyra by Likely7. This guide is also on the website:
https://dosukasol.github.io/PushFPS/#guide

1. START HERE: RECOMMENDED PUSHFPS SETTINGS
-------------------------------------------

Get the game on screen first (capture card at 60 FPS, even for a 30 FPS game: see section 4), then
set these in the Quick setup (Home or Pro mode):

  Console ................ your console (each console remembers its own choices)
  Game frame rate ........ 30 for most games (GTA VI), 40 for 40 FPS modes, 60 for performance modes
  Target frame rate ...... 60 FPS on a 60 Hz monitor, 120 FPS on 120 Hz and faster
                           (per refresh rate: "Which target for your monitor" below)
  Game preset ............ pick the game if it is listed (GTA VI has one): it sets the HUD boxes
  Smooth frame pacing .... ON (default): every generated frame is shown once
  Frame Pacing Repair .... OFF (default). Turn it on only if a game locked at 30 or 40 FPS still
                           judders: it evens out uneven frame delivery before frames are generated
  Live HUD Lock .......... ON: keeps text, crosshairs and the minimap sharp
  Crosshair Hold ......... ON for shooters (Quick setup, HUD): the crosshair is kept out of frame
                           generation, so it does not flicker or double when the camera moves
  Adaptive multiplier .... ON for games that dip below their frame rate
  AI picture enhancement . OFF to start (it uses a lot of GPU). Turn it on later if you have headroom
  Lowest latency ......... ON for action games. It changes nothing about picture quality

Then click "Optimise for my PC" with the game on screen (goal: Balanced). PushFPS tests the frame
generation methods your PC has on your real picture and keeps the best. "Undo" puts everything back.

Which target for your monitor
  Pick a target your monitor's refresh rate divides evenly, so every frame stays on screen equally
  long. That is why 120 beats 144: 144 is not a whole multiple of 30 or 60. "Match monitor" sets the
  target to your monitor's own rate.

  Monitor      30 FPS game (GTA VI)            60 FPS game                  Good to know
  60 Hz        60 (2x)                         Off                          Use Lowest latency only
  90 Hz        90 (3x)                         Off                          Offered in place of 120 on a 90 Hz screen
  75/100 Hz    60 (2x)                         Off                          G-SYNC/FreeSync on, or Windows at 60 Hz
  120 Hz       120 (4x)                        120 (2x)                     The sweet spot for console games
  144/165 Hz   120 (4x)                        120 (2x)                     G-SYNC/FreeSync on, or Windows at 120 Hz
  180 Hz       120 (4x) or Match monitor (6x)  Match monitor (3x)           180 = 6 x 30 = 3 x 60
  240 Hz       120 (4x) or Match monitor (8x)  120 (2x) or Match mon. (4x)  Start at 120
  360 Hz       120 (4x)                        120 (2x) or Match mon. (6x)  360 = 3 x 120

  Which multipliers your method offers:
    2x ......... every method
    3x and 4x .. NVIDIA Video Frame Generation, NVIDIA Frame Generation for games on RTX 50, ANR, Intel XeSS,
                 PushFlow (PushFPS's own, any DirectX 12 card)
    6x ......... NVIDIA Video Frame Generation, NVIDIA Frame Generation for games on RTX 50, ANR
    8x ......... NVIDIA Video Frame Generation only (RTX 40/50)
  AMD FSR stops at 2x, so a 30 FPS game goes to 60. More generated frames per real frame show more
  wobble in fast motion: if you see it, step down to 120 or 60. "Optimise for my PC" tests 120 and
  your monitor's rate when your PC can reach it, and keeps what passes.


2. THEN TUNE IT TO YOUR LIKING
------------------------------

Change one thing at a time, play a minute, then decide.

  If you see ...                              Try ...
  Crosshair flickers or doubles when the      Crosshair Hold on (Quick setup, HUD)
  camera moves
  Smeared HUD or text in fast motion          Live HUD Lock on, use the game preset or Automatic HUD
                                              detection, or a lower target (60 instead of 120)
  Wobbly edges or artifacts in fast motion    A lower target, or a higher frame generation quality in Pro mode
  Stutter or uneven motion                    Smooth frame pacing on, G-SYNC/FreeSync on in your driver,
                                              capture card set to exactly 60 FPS, fullscreen (F11)
  The game feels slow to react                Lowest latency on, 120 Hz console output (if your capture
                                              card captures 120 Hz), or Target "Off" for competitive games
  Ghosting or "jelly" with a 30 FPS game      On an RTX 40/50: NVIDIA Video Frame Generation, quality
                                              Medium to start, High at 2x if your card keeps up (section 4)
  Soft or blocky picture                      RTX Video Super Resolution at Medium (the default) on
                                              NVIDIA, NVIDIA Image Scaling on any card, or AI picture
                                              enhancement (NVIDIA RTX, Radeon RX 7000/9000; needs the
                                              installer's experimental extras: run Install-PushFPS.cmd
                                              again and answer y when it asks)
  Frame rate drops with AI enhancement on     AI enhancement off, or a lower target
  An AMD or Intel card cannot keep up (FSR)   "Check my setup" on the home page, then "Use the lighter
                                              motion estimate" (Undo puts it back)
  Washed-out or too dark picture              Match the colour settings in the capture dialog to the
                                              console: HDR or SDR, Limited or Full range
  Black picture from a PlayStation            Turn HDCP off on the PS5 (see below)

Want the best picture rather than the lowest delay? Use "Optimise for my PC" with the goal "Best
picture". Want the lowest delay? Goal "Lowest latency" plus the Lowest latency switch.


3. CONSOLE SETTINGS
-------------------

All consoles
  - Connect the console's HDMI out to the capture card's input, and the card to a USB 3 port directly
    on the PC (or a PCIe slot).
  - Output 60 Hz or 120 Hz: this depends on your capture card, not on your console (see below).
  - Variable refresh rate (VRR) OFF on the console. Capture needs a steady 60 or 120 Hz signal.
  - Resolution: what your capture card captures at 60 FPS (4K if it does 4K60, otherwise 1080p or 1440p).
  - In-game: a mode locked to 30 FPS gives the sharpest picture, and PushFPS adds the smoothness.
    A 60 FPS performance mode gives less delay.

60 or 120 Hz? It depends on your capture card (same on PS5, PS5 Pro, Xbox Series X|S, Switch 2)
  Your capture card                                   Console output   Why
  Captures 120 Hz at your resolution                  120 Hz           Each frame reaches the PC about
    (e.g. Elgato 4K X; Elgato 4K S at 1080p/1440p)                     8 ms sooner; 40 FPS modes work
  Captures 60 Hz (most cards, many cards at 4K, and   60 Hz            The card can't capture more
    cards that only pass 120 Hz through to your TV,
    like the Elgato HD60 X)
  Not sure                                            60 Hz first      Then try 120 Hz and check the
                                                                       picture stays steady
  - Look at your card's capture rates, not its passthrough rates. With passthrough, the console may
    still pick 120 Hz from your TV even though the card can't capture it: you then get a black
    picture or stutter. Set 60 Hz by hand in that case.
  - 120 Hz doesn't make the game run faster: a 30 FPS game stays 30 FPS, so the game frame rate in
    PushFPS stays 30.

PlayStation 5 and PS5 Pro
  - Settings > System > HDMI > Enable HDCP: OFF (required, otherwise the capture card shows black).
  - Settings > Screen and Video > Video Output > Resolution: match your capture card.
  - 120 Hz Output: Automatic only if your capture card captures 120 Hz (see above); otherwise Off.
  - VRR: Off.
  - HDR: see section 6. "On When Supported" for HDR play, "Off" for streaming and SDR clips.
  - RGB range: Automatic, and match it in PushFPS's capture dialog (Limited or Full).

Xbox Series X|S
  - Settings > General > TV & display options:
      Resolution: match your capture card.
      Refresh rate: 60 Hz (120 Hz only if your capture card captures 120 Hz, see above).
  - Video modes: Allow variable refresh rate OFF, Allow 24 Hz OFF. HDR10 and Auto HDR: see section 6.
  - Xbox games are not HDCP protected, so nothing else is needed.

Nintendo Switch
  - Docked. System Settings > TV Output: TV resolution 1080p, RGB range Automatic (match it in the
    capture dialog).

Nintendo Switch 2
  - Docked, 60 Hz output. TV resolution 1080p, or 4K if your capture card captures 4K60.
  - HDR: see section 6.
  - Switch games are not HDCP protected.

Videos on your PC (YouTube in a browser, video apps, files): use Video playback mode (the play
button in the top bar). Pick the window, switch on Smoother and Sharper, press F11.

No capture card? Streaming sources work too (PS5 Remote Play, Moonlight from a Sunshine PC, Xbox):
section 8 sets up Remote Play step by step.
For PS5 Remote Play pick 1080p 60 FPS with H.265 (the default for new pairings). On a wired network
a requested bitrate of 30 Mbps or more gives a sharper picture; lower it if the picture stutters.


4. CAPTURE CARD SETTINGS
------------------------

With thanks to Likely7, Veyra's developer: the delay figures and card picks in this section are his,
checked against the makers' specs and corrected where needed.

  Frame rate ...... 60 FPS, also for a 30 FPS game. Each new picture reaches the PC sooner than with
                    a 30 FPS capture. Half the frames are then repeats (AABBCC), so PushFPS removes
                    them before generating frames: the Quick setup and "Optimise for my PC" do this
                    for you; in Pro mode it is "Remove duplicate frames" (Automatic removal or 60->30).
                    120 FPS only if your card captures 120 Hz (section 3).
  Format .......... uncompressed NV12 (SDR) or P010 (HDR). Avoid MJPEG where you can: it is
                    compressed and has to be decoded. On a 5 Gbps card, 4K60 only exists as MJPEG
                    (the Elgato 4K S, for example).
  Lowest delay .... capture 1440p60 NV12 and let RTX Video Super Resolution (Medium) or NVIDIA Image
                    Scaling upscale it. HDR monitor but no HDR capture? RTX Video HDR in Pro mode
                    turns the SDR picture into HDR (NVIDIA RTX).

Delay of the capture step (measured by Veyra's developer; your card can differ a little)
  1440p 60 FPS NV12 ........ 10-20 ms   the lowest; works on 5 Gbps cards
  4K 60 FPS NV12 ........... 30-40 ms   needs a 10 Gbps card on a 10 Gbps port, or PCIe
  4K 30 FPS P010 (HDR) ..... 80-100 ms  avoid for playing: most delay, only 30 pictures a second.
                                        4K60 HDR needs a PCIe card
  Cards with the same chip differ by about 5 ms or less, whatever the brand.

Which capture card
  USB 5 Gbps ... uncompressed 1440p60 and 4K30 NV12; 1080p60 HDR on some.
                 Elgato HD60 X, Elgato 4K S (4K60 only as MJPEG), AVerMedia GC553 Pro / GC551G2,
                 MICHENSEN T800 / T900, KUHAIMI 27P / 26P, ACASIS 007 Pro / 009 Pro
  USB 10 Gbps .. uncompressed 4K60 NV12, 1440p60 HDR, 4K30 HDR.
                 Elgato 4K X, AVerMedia GC553G2 (both also capture 120 Hz), MICHENSEN T911,
                 KUHAIMI G50. Plug into a real 10 Gbps port (USB 3.2 Gen 2) on the PC: on 5 Gbps
                 they capture less.
  PCIe ......... 4K60 HDR. AVerMedia GC573, Elgato 4K Pro
  Tested with PushFPS: Elgato 4K X, HD60 X and 4K S. Reported working by users: Elgato HD60 S,
  Elgato HD60 Pro, AVerMedia Live Gamer Ultra 2.1 (GC553G2), AVerMedia Live Streamer Ultra HD
  (GC571), ezcap Game Link Raw, FEELWORLD C4K, the Monster Capture Card and the Magewell Pro
  Capture HDMI 4K Plus LT. The other models are Veyra's developer's picks.
  Any card that shows up as a camera in Windows works.

NVIDIA Video Frame Generation: Medium or High?
  For 30 FPS games it gives the least ghosting and wobble of the methods (RTX 40/50; RTX 30
  experimental). High is the cleanest but heavy: Veyra's developer measured about 20 ms per
  generated 4K frame on an RTX 5070. That fits 2x (one new frame per 33 ms game frame) but not 4x,
  which needs three. Medium costs about a third to a quarter of High and still beats the other
  methods at 30 FPS, so it is the default. High+Medium uses High only for the middle frame of each
  pair. At 2x, "VFG auto regulation" drops a pair to the next tier when it can't keep up instead
  of dropping frames. All in Pro mode under Frame generation, or let "Optimise for my PC" test
  what your card keeps up with.

PushFlow: which tier?
  PushFlow is PushFPS's own frame generation (RIFE through DirectML) and runs on NVIDIA, AMD and
  Intel cards, 2x to 4x. Start with Balanced. Fast estimates motion at half resolution for slower
  cards; Quality uses the full model and is the heaviest. Let "Optimise for my PC" check what your
  card keeps up with.


5. WINDOWS SETTINGS
-------------------

  Hardware-accelerated GPU scheduling ... ON  (Settings > System > Display > Graphics > Advanced
                                               graphics settings). Required for NVIDIA's frame
                                               generation; on by default in Windows 11. Restart after
                                               changing it.
  Monitor refresh rate .................. the highest your monitor has (Settings > System > Display >
                                          Advanced display)
  G-SYNC / FreeSync ..................... ON in the NVIDIA Control Panel or AMD Software, for
                                          fullscreen and windowed mode
  Windows HDR ........................... see section 6
  Game Mode ............................. ON (the Windows default)
  Graphics driver ....................... up to date
  Power ................................. laptops: plugged in, "Best performance" power mode
  Microphone (for recordings/streams) ... Settings > Privacy & security > Microphone > allow desktop
                                          apps to access your microphone
  Other apps ............................ close OBS (unless you use the PushFPS OBS plugin) and the
                                          capture card's own software before connecting
  PushFPS window ........................ play fullscreen (F11): it skips the desktop compositor


6. WHEN TO USE HDR
------------------

HDR only works end to end when every step is HDR: console, capture card, Windows and your monitor.
One SDR step means SDR.

  What you do                          Console HDR   Windows HDR   PushFPS
  -----------------------------------  -----------   -----------   ---------------------------------------
  Play on an HDR monitor               On            On            Default (HDR output). Capture card set
  (capture card captures HDR)                                      to HDR at 60 FPS (1080p or 1440p on
                                                                   USB cards; 4K60 HDR needs PCIe). No
                                                                   HDR capture: RTX Video HDR (NVIDIA)
  Play on an SDR monitor               Off           Off           Default
  Record clips to share (Discord,      Off           Off           SDR recording (H.264). Or keep the
  TikTok, Shorts, Reels, most editors)                             console on HDR and turn on "Show as
                                                                   SDR" in the capture dialog
  Record in HDR (YouTube HDR,          On            On            Needs an NVIDIA graphics card: the file
  HDR video editing)                                               is HEVC 10-bit HDR
  Go live (Twitch, YouTube, Kick,      Off           Off           Streaming needs an SDR picture;
  TikTok)                                                          PushFPS asks you to turn HDR off first
  Vertical comparison clips            Off           Off           SDR gives a file every phone app shows
                                                                   correctly

Tips
  - If the picture looks grey or washed out, one step disagrees: check the console's HDR setting, the
    capture dialog's colour settings and Windows HDR.
  - Switching Windows HDR changes the whole desktop. Turn it on only for HDR play and HDR recordings.
  - Xbox Auto HDR adds HDR to SDR games: leave it off when you want SDR clips or streams.


7. QUICK CHECKLIST
------------------

  [ ] HDCP off (PlayStation)
  [ ] Console at 60 Hz (120 Hz only if your capture card captures 120 Hz), VRR off
  [ ] Capture card on a USB 3 port directly on the PC, 60 FPS (or 120), NV12 or P010, not MJPEG
  [ ] Quick setup: console, game frame rate 30, target from the monitor table (60 or 120), game preset
  [ ] Smooth frame pacing and Live HUD Lock on (Frame Pacing Repair only if a locked game judders)
  [ ] Optimise for my PC with the game on screen
  [ ] Lowest latency on for action games
  [ ] G-SYNC/FreeSync on, monitor at its highest refresh rate, fullscreen (F11)
  [ ] HDR only when every step is HDR; SDR for streams and shared clips

8. REMOTE PLAY SETUP (PS5 AND XBOX, WITHOUT A CAPTURE CARD)
-----------------------------------------------------------

Console and PC on the same home network. You set it up once.

PS5

  On your PS5
  1. Turn Remote Play on: Settings > System > Remote Play > Enable Remote Play.
  2. Get the pairing number: Settings > System > Remote Play > Pair Device (older system software
     calls it Link Device). The PS5 shows 8 digits for 5 minutes. Leave them on screen.

  In PushFPS
  3. Open PS5 streaming and press Search. It finds your PS5 and fills in its address. Nothing found?
     Type the IP address the PS5 shows under Settings > Network > Connection Status > View
     Connection Status.
  4. Press Get Account ID. Sony's own sign-in page opens in your browser. Sign in, copy the address
     the browser ends on (it starts with https://remoteplay.dl.playstation.net) into the box under it
     and press Fill in: PushFPS fills in your Account ID. Your PSN name itself does not work.
     Never paste an "NPSSO" code anywhere: it is your signed-in PSN session.
  5. Type the 8 digits and press Pair and save.
  6. Press Connect. If the PS5 asks for the sign-in PIN of your PS5 user, type it under Sign-in PIN
     in the same window.

  Controller: connect it to the PC with a USB cable, or pair it with the PC's Bluetooth. PushFPS
  passes it on to the PS5 while its window is in front.

  You pair once. Next time: open PS5 streaming and press Connect. To start a PS5 that is in rest
  mode with the Wake button, also turn on Stay Connected to the Internet and Enable Turning On PS5
  from Network under Settings > System > Power Saving > Features Available in Rest Mode.

Xbox (unofficial and experimental: it can stop working when Microsoft changes its service)

  On your Xbox
  1. Turn remote features on: Settings > Devices & connections > Remote features > Enable remote
     features.
  2. Set the power option to Sleep: Settings > General > Power options > Sleep.

  In PushFPS
  3. Open Xbox streaming and press Sign in. PushFPS shows a code. Enter it at microsoft.com/link and
     sign in with the Microsoft account your Xbox uses. PushFPS never sees your password.
  4. Pick your console and press Start streaming. Not in the list? Press Refresh. The Xbox has to be
     signed in with the same account.
  5. Use this PC's controller: connect it to the PC (USB cable, wireless adapter or Bluetooth) and
     leave the Controller switch on.

Not working?

  The PS5 will not pair
  - Account ID comes from Get Account ID (Sony's sign-in, step 4), not your PSN name.
  - The 8 digits: no space, and a new number once 5 minutes have passed.
  - No address? Press Search, or type the PS5's IP address.
  - PS5 and PC on the same network: no guest Wi-Fi, no VPN. If Windows asks whether PushFPS may use
    the network, allow it.

  The controller does nothing
  - Connect it to the PC, not to the console.
  - Keep PushFPS in front: click its window once. It only passes the controller on while it is the
    active window.
  - PS5: View only off. Xbox: Controller on.
  - More than one controller or wheel connected? Unplug the others and connect again.

  The Xbox is not in the list
  - Sign in on the console with the same Microsoft account.
  - Remote features on, power option Sleep, then press Refresh.

  It stutters, drops out or closes
  - Use a network cable for the console, and for the PC if you can.
  - PS5: lower Requested bitrate, or set Decoding to CPU software.
  - Xbox: turn on Software video decoding.
