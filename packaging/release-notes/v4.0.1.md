## What's new in 4.0.1

4.0.1 takes back the two things in 4.0 that changed the picture in motion. Within hours of 4.0 we heard,
and saw ourselves, that the picture had become softer in motion, that the edges of moving things and of the
character broke up when the camera turned, that small moving things ghosted and that thin things such as
poles cut in and out. Going back to 3.2.10 made it go away. With 4.0.1 frame generation is handed the same
frames, and its result is left alone, exactly as in 3.2.10. Everything else from 4.0 stays.

### 60 → 30 hands on every game frame again

For people who use duplicate removal on *60 → 30* (a 30 FPS game through a 60 Hz capture).

4.0 skipped a sample when it looked like a repeat of the picture before it, so that a game dipping under 30
would not be shown as pairs of identical frames. But "looks like a repeat" comes from a comparison that has
to tolerate a capture card's noise, and small motion (a slight turn of the camera, a small thing moving, an
idle animation) stays inside that tolerance for a frame or two. Those were real game frames, and 4.0 threw
them away. Frame generation then had to bridge two game frames at exactly those places.

Simulated afterwards with the 3.2.10 and the 4.0 code on a steady 30 FPS game: where the comparison noticed
90 % of the new pictures, 4.0 handed on 27 frames a second instead of 30; where it noticed 50 to 70 %, 22 to
24, with 6 to 8 pairs every second that were two game frames apart. 3.2.10 hands on 30, every game frame.

- 4.0.1 uses 3.2.10's selection again, decision for decision.
- A game that dips under 30 shows a repeated pair now and then, as it did in 3.2.10.
- *Automatic removal*, which most people use, was not changed in 4.0 and is not changed now.

### Keep the HUD sharp is off unless you switch it on

4.0 switched **Keep the HUD sharp** on for everyone. It keeps a pixel as the console drew it when the pixel
is the same in two frames in a row. That is true of a HUD, but also, by chance, of a great many pixels in a
moving game picture (road, walls, skin, anything smooth or motion blurred). Those were held back while the
rest of the picture moved. Measured afterwards on moving test pictures of the kind real games produce, in
which nothing stands still, it had overwritten between 5 and 38 % of every generated frame. The drawn test
pictures it was first measured on did not show this.

- From 4.0.1 the option is **off** for everyone who has not switched it on themselves.
- It is still in *Quick setup → Keep the HUD sharp*, with a line that says what it can do to a moving picture.
- For a HUD, the game presets, HUD protection and Live HUD Lock are unchanged. They know where the HUD is,
  or watch it over time, and leave the rest of the picture alone.

If you switched the option off yourself in 4.0, nothing changes for you.

### How this was checked

Both causes were found by reading the code and shown with numbers: the first by running the 3.2.10 and the
4.0 selection side by side on simulated captures, the second by running the feature's own rule on generated
moving pictures. The automatic tests ran in the build pipeline on a software renderer. Neither was measured
by us on a real graphics card or capture card. One thing from 4.0 still differs from 3.2.10 in the picture:
on an HDR output, a display that reports its signal's ceiling as its peak no longer scales the tone curve.
